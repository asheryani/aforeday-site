What it does

Three jobs a good chief of staff does, and nothing else.

  1. Promises you made, before they cost you.

    Purser reads the mail you sent yourself for the commitments in it — “I’ll send that by Friday” — and brings back the ones that are due with the sentence you wrote them in. When it is not sure a sentence is a promise, it asks you first.

  2. A calendar that pushes back.

    Purser tells the meetings you chose apart from the meetings booked onto you, and drafts the decline or the request to move it, for you to review and send from your mail app.

  3. Three things, with consequences.

    Open Purser in the morning and you get a shortlist of what needs you today, and what happens if you skip it. Not a summary of your day. When nothing needs you, Purser says so and stays quiet.

Your commitments and meetings, in one line.

The timeline puts what you promised, who you are waiting on and where you need to be side by side, so the day reads in the order it will happen.

Ask what needs you.

The assistant answers from your own calendar, reminders and mail, and its answers link back to the items they came from, so you can check them rather than trust them.

Quiet by default, and under your control.

Choose how much a notification reveals on your Lock Screen, and decide how Purser acts: suggest everything first, or let it take care of reversible things like adding a reminder, and tell you so you can undo it.

Purser never sends mail. A draft opens in your own mail app, and you send it.

Your Google account

What Purser does with your Google data.

Connecting Google is optional; Purser works with your iPhone’s calendar and reminders without it. If you connect, Purser asks only for the permissions below, explains each one in the app before Google’s consent screen, and you can disconnect at any time in Settings › Connected accounts.

Every Google permission Purser can request.

PermissionWhat it is forWhat Purser keeps
Basic profileopenid email profile Knowing which Google account you connected, and signing you in if you choose Sign in with Google. The account identifier and email address, and your name if you sign in with Google.
Google Calendar, read-onlycalendar.readonly Showing your Google Calendar meetings in the timeline and preparing briefs for them. Your calendars’ names, and for the ones you select: each event’s title, description, times, organiser, attendees and link. Purser cannot create, change or delete events.
Gmail metadatagmail.metadata Noticing conversations where someone is waiting on you, or you are waiting on them. A summary per conversation: the addresses involved, who wrote last and when, and the follow-up cards built from it. No subjects, no message text.
Gmail read-onlygmail.readonly Finding the promises in mail you sent yourself, so you are reminded before they are overdue. Used only for messages you sent, at most 25 per sync. The promise, the sentence it came from, who it was to, the due date and a link back to the message. The message itself is never stored.

What Purser never does. It never sends, deletes, labels or changes anything in your Google account. It never reads mail you received to look for promises. It never uses your data for advertising, never sells it, and does not use it to train AI models. Purser’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The details, including how long each kind of data is kept, are in the privacy policy.

Privacy you can check

Built to be trusted with your working life.

Only what the feature needs

Every permission maps to a feature you can see, and the app explains each one before Google’s consent screen.

Messages are read, not kept

Mail text is analysed in memory and discarded. What stays is the promise sentence, not the message.

AI in the EU, never trained on you

AI runs on Google Cloud in Belgium (europe-west1), is not used to train models, and can be switched off.

Export or delete, from the app

Download a copy of your data, or delete your account, from Settings. The policy lists exactly what each covers.

Your Lock Screen, your rules

Notifications from your phone show full details, a discreet summary, or only that Purser has something.

You send, Purser drafts

Purser has no permission to send anything. Drafts open in your mail app for you to review and send.
