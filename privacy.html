Privacy policy

For the Aforeday iPhone app and this website. Effective {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}}, version 3.0. It describes the app as it actually works today, not as we intend it to work.

The short version

  • You sign in with Apple or Google. Aforeday reads the calendars and reminder lists you choose on your iPhone and, only if you connect them, the other accounts listed in section 2, each only as far as that section says.

2. What Aforeday processes

Your account

You sign in with Sign in with Apple or with Google. With Apple, we store the email address Apple gives us (this may be a private relay address if you choose to hide yours), your name if Apple provides it on your first sign-in, and Apple’s account identifier for Aforeday.

With Google, the app asks Google only for your basic profile (the permissions openid, email and profile). Your phone completes the sign-in with Google itself and sends us only Google’s signed confirmation of who you are. From it we store your Google account identifier, your email address and your name. We never receive or keep a Google token, so signing in with Google gives Aforeday no access to your Gmail, your calendar or anything else in your Google account. If Google confirms your address and it matches an Aforeday account you already have, the Google sign-in is added to that account instead of creating a second one.

For every sign-in we keep a record of the device: model, iOS version, app version and when it was last seen. Sign-in sessions are stored as a random token, kept only as a hash, valid 30 days, replaced each time it is used, and bound to the device it was issued to.

Your iPhone’s calendars and reminders

With your permission, the app reads the calendars and reminder lists you include — on first run, all visible ones — and uploads, for each event, its title, start and end, time zone, all-day flag and the display names of its attendees, and for each reminder its title and due date. Events from 90 days back to 60 days ahead are included, along with reminders that are open or were completed in the last 7 days.

Notes, event links and addresses are not uploaded. Organiser and attendee email addresses are reduced on the device to three yes/no facts: whether you organised the meeting, whether the organiser is outside your organisation, and whether your invitation is still unanswered. Short phrases derived from those facts, such as “booked by someone outside your organisation”, are uploaded as part of the cards Aforeday shows you.

Addresses you enter for travel planning, and the locations of your events, stay on your iPhone. They are sent from your phone to Apple Maps and Apple Weather to work out when to leave and whether weather will delay you; only the resulting “leave by” time and the meeting it belongs to reach us. The app never asks for your contacts, photos, camera or health data, and it asks for your location only if you turn on the setting described below. Voice input is transcribed on your device; the audio is never uploaded, and the text is sent only if you send it. If you turn on “Speak answers aloud”, the answer to a question you dictated is read aloud by your iPhone’s own speech voice, on the phone; nothing more is sent to us.

Your location, only if you allow it

If you turn on “Use my location for leave-by” in Settings › Travel, the app first explains what it will do and then asks iOS for permission to use your location while you use the app. It never asks for “Always” access. With that permission, the app reads where you are, on your iPhone, only in the three hours before a meeting that has a place, to work out when you need to leave. Your position is used on the phone and sent from it only to Apple Maps, to work out the travel time. It never reaches our servers, our logs or any analytics: only the resulting leave-by time and travel duration do. When you are within about 150 metres of the meeting’s place, the app treats you as arrived and marks the leave-by card done.

The app keeps no location history: it holds only the last position, on the phone, and discards it within 24 hours, when you turn the setting off or tap Forget now, when you sign out, and when you delete your account. Each time you turn the setting on or off, the app sends us a record of that choice (which version of the explanation you saw, and when), never a position. If you decline the iOS permission, nothing asks you again; leave-by times then come from your saved addresses and your previous meeting, as before.

Trips in your calendar

If one of your calendar events names a flight — an airline code, a flight number and a date, such as “LH 123 BER–MUC” — Aforeday recognises it as a trip. It does this on our servers with fixed patterns, not with AI, from the event’s title and, where a calendar provides one, its description; events from your iPhone and from Outlook have no description on our servers, so in practice it is the title. If you have connected Outlook mail, mail from that airline’s sender domain around the same day can make Aforeday more confident that the trip is real; only the sender’s domain and the date are used, never the subject or the text.

For each trip we store the airline and flight number, the departure date, the airports and scheduled times where the event gives them, how confident the detection is, where it came from, and your answer if you told Aforeday whether it is your flight. In the three days before departure, Aforeday may show you a card about the trip; when it is less sure, the card asks whether the flight is yours. If you tap “Not my flight”, we keep the airline and flight number so that the same flight is not suggested again. You can see detected trips, or switch the feature off, under Settings › Travel › Flight alerts; it is on by default, and switching it off erases your trips at the next daily run.

Aforeday does not look up flight status today (section 5): it does not know about gate changes, delays or cancellations, and no airline or flight-data service is told about your trip.

Accounts you connect

In Settings › Connected accounts you can connect accounts you hold with other services. Connecting is always your choice. Before you are handed to the service, the app shows what Aforeday will ask it for and why. You then sign in at the service itself, never in Aforeday, and the service gives our servers an access token. Those tokens stay on our servers, encrypted (section 11); they never reach your phone. For each connected account we keep which service it is, the name or address it shows, the permissions it granted, its status and when it was connected, and we record every permission granted or withdrawn in your consent history. You can disconnect at any time (section 10).

Each service below is a separate company’s service, under its own terms and privacy policy; Aforeday reads from it on your instruction and changes nothing there unless this section says so.

Outlook Calendar

Aforeday asks Microsoft for Calendars.ReadBasic, plus openid, profile, email and offline_access, which tell us which Microsoft account you connected and keep the connection working without asking you to sign in every hour. Calendars.ReadBasic is read-only and cannot return an event’s notes, body or attachments. From the calendars you select, Aforeday reads events from 30 days back to 60 days ahead and stores, for each, its title, start and end, time zone, all-day flag, whether it was cancelled, the email addresses of its organiser and attendees, and its link in Outlook. Unlike events from your iPhone, Outlook events reach our servers with their attendees’ addresses, because the reading happens on our servers. When an event also appears in another calendar you use, Aforeday shows it once. Microsoft can tell our servers that something changed; those messages carry no event data and only prompt a fresh read. Outlook events are used like your other calendar events: in your timeline, reminders, meeting briefs and the assistant (section 4).

Outlook mail

Outlook mail is a separate connection with its own permission request. Aforeday asks Microsoft for Mail.ReadBasic (with the same openid, profile, email and offline_access), which shows who emailed whom and when. Microsoft refuses message bodies, previews, attachments and search under this permission, and Aforeday never asks for the subject. From your Inbox and Sent Items, Aforeday reads each message’s sender, recipients, dates and conversation, and, for some messages, the technical headers that mark mailing lists and automated mail (never the subject). From these it keeps, per conversation: the addresses of the people in it, who wrote last and when, whether you were a direct recipient, whether you have replied before, how many messages it has, whether it looks like bulk mail, the messages’ technical identifiers (so that the same email is recognised if it reaches you in two accounts), and a link to open it in Outlook. That is how Aforeday tells you that someone is waiting on your reply. The card names the person and how long they have waited.

GitHub

Aforeday asks GitHub for the notifications permission only, which lists your GitHub notifications and cannot change anything on GitHub or read code. It keeps three kinds: a review requested from you, a mention of you, and an issue assigned to you; every other notification is dropped as soon as it is read. For each it stores the kind, the repository’s name, the kind of item, its page on github.com, when it changed, whether you have read it, and its title, cleaned of invisible characters and cut to 120 characters. Aforeday never reads code, issue text, pull request descriptions or comments. The card names the repository; the item’s title is shown to you on the card but never sent to AI (section 4), because anyone can write it.

Linear

Aforeday asks Linear for read, its narrowest permission, which cannot create, change or comment on anything. It reads the unfinished issues assigned to you and stores, for each, its key (such as “ENG-42”), its title cut to 120 characters, its due date, state, priority, project name and link. It never reads descriptions or comments. The card names the issue by its key; the title is shown to you but never sent to AI (section 4).

Jira

Aforeday asks Atlassian for read:jira-work and read:jira-user, which let it find the Jira issues assigned to you, read:me, which names your Atlassian account in Settings, and offline_access, which keeps the connection working. All are read-only. On each Jira site you give it access to, it reads your issues that are not done and stores, for each, its key, its summary cut to 120 characters, its due date, status, priority, project name and link. It never reads descriptions or comments. The card names the issue by its key; the summary is shown to you but never sent to AI (section 4).

Asana

Aforeday asks Asana for tasks:read, users:read and workspaces:read: it reads your name, which workspaces you belong to, and the open tasks assigned to you in each. All are read-only. For each task it stores its name cut to 120 characters, its due date, whether it is complete, and its link. It never reads task notes or comments. The card says which service the task is in; the task’s name is shown to you but never sent to AI (section 4).

Notion

When you connect Notion, Notion asks you which pages and databases to share with Aforeday; Aforeday sees only those, never the rest of your workspace. Aforeday can read content and your own Notion name and identifier, not your email address, and cannot change, add or comment on anything. In the databases you shared, it reads the tasks assigned to you that have a due date: their title, who they are assigned to, their due date and whether they are done. It never reads the text inside a page. It stores, for each such task, its title (cut to 200 characters), its due date and its link, and for each shared database its title, which of its fields hold the assignee, due date and status, and when it was last read. A task that is done, reassigned, archived or no longer due is removed at the next read, and so is everything from a database you stop sharing. Task titles appear on your cards as your reminders do, and can reach AI like any card title (section 4).

HubSpot

Aforeday asks HubSpot for crm.objects.deals.read, crm.objects.contacts.read and crm.objects.owners.read. All are read-only; Aforeday never creates or changes anything in HubSpot. It reads your deals (name, stage, amount, close date, owner), your contacts (name, email address, company, website and when you last had contact) and which HubSpot user is you. It stores, for each deal: its name and link, its stage as one of open, closing, won or lost, its amount, currency and close date, whether you own it, when there was last activity, and its contacts’ email addresses; and for each contact: their email address, name, company and company domain, the kind and time of your last contact, and how important the relationship looks. A deal’s amount is stored encrypted. HubSpot can tell our servers that a deal or contact changed; we use that only to read again, and keep nothing from the message.

Salesforce

Aforeday asks Salesforce for api, refresh_token and openid. Salesforce has no narrower permission for reading records, so the limit is what Aforeday asks for: your opportunities (name, stage, amount, close date, owner), your contacts (name, email address, company, website, last activity), and the dates of your calls, emails and meetings with them — never their subject or description — plus which Salesforce user and organisation you connected. Aforeday never writes to Salesforce. It stores the same facts as for HubSpot: per deal its name and link, stage, amount (encrypted), currency, close date, whether you own it, last activity and its contacts’ email addresses; per contact their email address, name, company and domain, the kind and time of your last contact, and how important the relationship looks.

How your CRM affects what Aforeday shows

With HubSpot or Salesforce connected, Aforeday can show a card when a deal you own needs you — it is closing, or it has gone quiet with its close date near. The card names the deal. It can also move a mail follow-up from someone your CRM knows a little higher in your list, by a fixed maximum; your CRM never makes Aforeday interrupt you with a notification on its own, and if you mark that person as not important, the CRM stops counting for them. You can switch all of this off with “Let your CRM shape what comes first” in Settings › Connected accounts; with it off, nothing from your CRM changes the order and no deal cards are shown.

Slack

Aforeday asks Slack, for your own user account and no bot, for im:read and im:history, which list your direct-message conversations and their messages, and users:read, which turns Slack user ids into names. It reads direct messages only — never channels or group conversations. For each direct-message conversation it stores who wrote last and when, the other person’s Slack id and display name, whether they are a bot, and whether you have replied before; nobody’s message text is stored there. That is how Aforeday tells you that someone is waiting on your reply. The card names the person.

Aforeday also reads the messages you wrote yourself in your direct messages in the last 7 days, to notice promises you made — “I’ll send it Friday” — and remind you before they are overdue. This uses AI (section 4) and only happens while AI and “Email content to AI” are on. Of each message, only the sentence with the promise is kept, with who it was to and when it is due. The other person’s messages are not read for this.

If you turn on Aforeday’s agent and allow it to read Slack, it also reads the other person’s messages in the direct-message conversation a card is about — one conversation at a time, at most the 15 newest messages — to draft a reply for you to review. Before anything is used, hidden text, sign-in codes and secrets are removed and links are reduced to the website’s name. What it reads exists in memory for that one run and is never written to our database or logs; what is kept is the draft you review.

If you let Aforeday send Slack replies, Slack asks you to approve one more permission, chat:write, for your own account. Aforeday uses it only to post a reply you approved, or one you told Aforeday to send for you, as you, into an existing direct-message conversation. It never posts in channels or group conversations, never starts a conversation with someone new, and never edits or deletes your messages. Just before it sends, it reads that conversation once more to check that nobody has written since; if someone has, it does not send. Nothing it reads there is stored.

Telegram, only if you link it

If you link Telegram in Settings › Connected accounts › Chat, you can ask Aforeday things and hear from it in a private chat with Aforeday’s bot, which is an AI assistant. We keep the Telegram chat identifier that lets the bot write to you, the first name Telegram shows us, and the level of detail you chose for the chat, which starts as your notification preview setting. We never ask for your phone number. What you write to the bot is handled like your assistant conversations in the app (section 4). If a message you send looks like a password, a one-time code, an API key, or a card or bank account number, Aforeday stores it with that part removed, does not send it to AI, and tries to delete your message in Telegram. Aforeday never asks for a password or code in a chat.

The bot writes to you with answers to what you ask and with notices of what Aforeday has prepared, following the level of detail you chose: at the fullest level a notice can include the card’s title and the exact text of a proposed reminder or draft; at the others, less or only that something is waiting. Messages Aforeday starts follow your quiet hours, mutes and Pause, and the same daily limit as notifications.

What you create in Aforeday

The cards Aforeday shows you and what you do with them (shown, done, dismissed, snoozed, and when); your conversations with the assistant, including the replies and the items they cite; drafts you ask for, such as a note declining or moving a meeting, including any edits you make; meeting briefs and daily briefings; promises Aforeday found in your messages and whether you kept them; what the agent proposed and your decision on each (section 3); and your settings, including how Aforeday may act, what a notification may reveal, and your AI choices.

Face ID and your passcode

Before Aforeday widens what it may do on its own (section 3), the app asks iOS to confirm it is you, with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. That check is made by iOS on your device. Aforeday receives only whether it succeeded; no biometric data exists in the app or reaches our servers.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, we store your device’s push tokens. Every notification and Lock Screen activity we send follows the preview mode you choose in Settings — full details, a discreet summary, or only that Aforeday has something for you — which the app sends to us so our servers can honour it. We keep a record of each push we send — its type, status and time, never its text — and, for the reminders before a meeting, the meeting’s title and time. We also send empty background pushes with no text and no identifiers, so a change you make on one device reaches your other devices sooner.

Your subscription

Aforeday requires a subscription, bought through Apple’s App Store. Apple processes the payment; we never see your card, billing address or Apple ID password. From Apple we receive signed transaction information and store the subscription’s product, status, expiry, whether it renews, and Apple’s transaction identifier. Apple also sends us notifications when a subscription changes; we keep a content-free record of each: its type, Apple’s identifiers and when it was processed.

Diagnostics

When the app crashes, or dictation fails, it writes a technical note — the kind of failure, the exception or signal, a reason, up to 64 lines of the app’s own call stack, the build number, and for a dictation failure the kind of audio input in use, such as a Bluetooth headset — and sends it the next time you open the app. Never your events, your messages or your transcript, and never a device’s name. This is on by default, is disclosed on the first screen of the app, and can be switched off under Settings › Diagnostics › Send crash reports; switched off, nothing is sent and a note already waiting is deleted. These notes go into our server logs, not our database. Our logs otherwise hold internal identifiers, counts, error descriptions and, for requests we throttle, IP addresses. Aforeday’s code does not write the content of your calendar, your conversations or your connected accounts to its logs. The app contains no analytics or crash-reporting service and no third-party code.

3. The agent and your approvals

Aforeday can work on what it shows you. It is off until you turn it on in Settings › Aforeday’s agent. Once on, it proposes drafts and small actions from your calendar, reminders, the accounts you connected and the cards it has built from them, either on its own or when you tap a card. Every proposal waits on the Now screen until you approve, edit or skip it.

  • Ask me first is the default. Each kind of action has its own setting, “Ask me first” or “Act and tell me”, under Settings › Aforeday’s agent › What Aforeday may do on its own. Choosing “Act and tell me” for a kind of action that can leave your phone asks for Face ID or your passcode first. The setting is yours: the AI never sees or changes it.
  • Reversible things run at once, with undo. Under “Act and tell me”, an action you can undo inside the app, such as adding a reminder to your iPhone, runs and tells you.
  • Anything that leaves your phone is held first. Under “Act and tell me”, a message to someone else is announced by a notification with an Undo button and sent only after a five-minute window. A first message to someone you have never written to from that account always asks you in the app, with Face ID or your passcode, whatever the setting. Today the one place Aforeday can send a message for you is Slack: a reply, as you, in an existing direct-message conversation, if you connected Slack and allowed it to send (section 2). If Aforeday cannot confirm that a reply went out, it tells you and does not try again on its own.
  • What is kept. For each proposal: what kind of action it was, the reminder title or the draft it referred to, your decision, how you approved it, and the result. For a Slack reply Aforeday sent, it also keeps Slack’s reference to the message (the conversation and the message’s timestamp); the reply’s text exists only as the draft you reviewed. We also keep a record of each time the agent worked for you — when, which card, how many steps, the cost and why it stopped, with no text — linked to your account for 30 days.
  • In a linked chat. If you linked Telegram, Aforeday can tell you there that a proposal is waiting, with the detail you chose for the chat. Approving still happens in the app: the chat message carries no approve button, and nothing is done because of a reply in the chat.
  • Pause. Settings › Aforeday’s agent › Pause stops everything the agent starts on its own: reading, drafting and proposing. Turning the agent off does the same.

4. AI processing

Aforeday uses Google’s Gemini 2.5 Flash model through Google Cloud Vertex AI, in Google’s europe-west1 region (Belgium). It is used for: writing your meeting briefs and daily briefings, answering you in the assistant and in a linked Telegram chat, writing the meeting decline or reschedule notes you ask for, finding promises in the Slack messages you wrote, and, if you turn the agent on, deciding what to propose and drafting it, including reading the Slack conversation a card is about if you allowed it.

What is sent is limited to what the feature needs: a meeting with up to ten earlier meetings involving the same people and your open items; the titles of your open items, your top cards and your upcoming events; or your own question. Card titles can name a person who is waiting on you, a repository, an issue key, a task or a deal. Never your whole calendar. For promises, one of your own Slack messages at a time. Before anything is sent, the length is capped and pattern filters replace common secret formats — labelled passwords and tokens, API keys, one-time codes, card- and IBAN-shaped numbers — with placeholders. Requests that exceed the limits are refused rather than trimmed.

Prompts and responses are not written to our logs. We keep a technical record of each AI call — the task, the model and prompt versions, token counts, cost, how long it took and whether it succeeded — with no content and no link to you.

Under our agreement with Google, this data is processed by Google as our service provider and is not used to train Google’s models. We do not use it to train any model of our own, and no part of our code trains or fine-tunes a model.

You can switch AI off entirely in Settings › Privacy & data › AI data controls, or switch off individual sources: calendar content, assistant conversations, and email content (“Email content to AI”, which also covers the Slack messages Aforeday reads and the agent’s drafting). With AI off, Aforeday falls back to its fixed rules, which is how it decides what needs your attention in any case; briefs show facts without a written narrative, and the agent drafts nothing.

5. Not available yet

Aforeday is built to do more than it does today. These are switched off on our servers, cannot be connected or turned on in the app, and no data from any of them is read, received or stored: Gmail, by any route; Google Calendar; WhatsApp; looking things up on the web, sending check-ins and holding restaurant tables for you; and flight status from a flight-data provider, so trips are recognised from your calendar but their gates, delays and cancellations are not looked up. Before any of them is switched on, this policy will be updated to describe exactly what it reads and keeps, and you will see a notice in the app (section 15).

6. Why, and on what legal basis

  • To provide the app — everything in sections 2 to 4, including the accounts you connect and the chat you link. Legal basis: performance of our contract with you, Article 6(1)(b) GDPR.
  • Other people in your data — the names and addresses of people who attend your meetings, write to you, appear in your messages or are your CRM contacts. We process them only as sections 2 to 4 describe, so that Aforeday can show you who is waiting on you and what matters, and we keep only what those features need. Aforeday writes to them only as you, with a reply you approved or told it to send (section 3). Legal basis: the legitimate interest, yours and ours, in providing the service you asked for, Article 6(1)(f) GDPR.
  • To keep the service secure and working — logs, crash notes, request throttling, abuse prevention. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in a secure, functioning service, Article 6(1)(f) GDPR.
  • To prove we honoured your choices — the consent and audit records described in section 9. Legal basis: our legal obligation to demonstrate compliance, Article 6(1)(c) with Article 5(2) GDPR.
  • To sell and service subscriptions — the subscription and App Store notification records. Legal basis: performance of our contract, and our legal obligations in accounting.

We do not use your data for advertising or profiling, we do not sell it, and we do not make automated decisions about you with legal or similarly significant effects.

7. Who else sees it

  • Google Cloud — as our processor, for hosting (Cloud Run), our database (Cloud SQL), short-lived queues and caches (Memorystore), AI processing (Vertex AI), secret storage, scheduling and logs.
  • Apple — for Sign in with Apple, push notifications and Live Activities, and the App Store when you subscribe. From your phone only: Apple Maps and Apple Weather for travel planning, on-device speech recognition and speech, and the Face ID or passcode check.
  • Google, for sign-in — if you sign in with Google, your phone talks to Google to sign you in, and Google confirms to us who you are. Google keeps its own record of that sign-in under its own privacy policy.
  • The services you connect — when Aforeday reads from an account you connected (section 2), that service sees which of its users connected Aforeday and what Aforeday asks it for. Each remains responsible for your data there, under its own terms. When Aforeday sends a Slack reply for you, Slack receives it and delivers it as yours.
  • Telegram — if you link it, the messages between you and Aforeday’s bot pass through, and are stored by, Telegram under its own privacy policy. Bot chats are not end-to-end encrypted. Aforeday can delete the messages it sent only within 48 hours; after that, only you can delete them in Telegram.
  • Where you send it yourself — apart from what section 3 describes, a draft leaves Aforeday only when you copy it or open it in your own mail app, and you send it.

There is no advertising network, no analytics provider, no crash-reporting service and no data broker in Aforeday. The app contains no third-party code at all.

8. Where it is processed

The app’s servers, database, caches and AI processing run in Google Cloud’s europe-west1 region in Belgium, and that is where what Aforeday reads from your connected accounts is stored and processed. Our application logs are kept in Google Cloud Logging, whose storage location is global rather than EU-only. Apple’s push, sign-in and App Store services, and the Maps and Weather requests made from your phone, are operated by Apple under its own terms and may involve processing outside the EU. The same is true of Google’s sign-in, the services you connect and Telegram: each is operated by its provider under its own terms, and several of them process data in the United States. Where a transfer outside the EU occurs, it takes place on the transfer mechanisms in our providers’ terms, such as the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses, or, for a service you connected yourself, on that service’s own.

10. Disconnecting, exporting and deleting

Disconnecting an account

In Settings › Connected accounts. Disconnecting stops every read at once, destroys our copy of the account’s access tokens, cancels anything the agent was waiting to do with that account (an action already under way is allowed to finish), and erases what Aforeday kept from the account — the events, facts, items, tasks, deals, contacts or promises section 2 describes — and the cards built from it, on every device. We keep the record that the account was connected (section 9) until you delete your account.

Where the service offers it, we also ask it to withdraw Aforeday’s permission, so the account no longer lists Aforeday:

  • Outlook — Microsoft offers no way for us to withdraw it. Aforeday can no longer read anything, but the permission stays listed in your Microsoft account, among the apps that have access to it, until you remove it there.
  • GitHub — withdrawn at GitHub when you disconnect.
  • Linear — withdrawn at Linear when you disconnect.
  • Jira — Atlassian offers no way for us to withdraw it. The permission stays listed in your Atlassian account’s connected apps until you remove it there.
  • Asana — we cannot withdraw it ourselves; our last access lapses within an hour and cannot be renewed. The app stays listed in your Asana settings until you remove it there.
  • Notion — withdrawn at Notion when you disconnect.
  • HubSpot — withdrawn at HubSpot when you disconnect.
  • Salesforce — withdrawn at Salesforce when you disconnect.
  • Slack — withdrawn at Slack when you disconnect. Any reply still waiting to be sent is cancelled.

Unlinking a chat

In Settings › Connected accounts › Chat, or by sending /unlink to the bot. This deletes the link and the chat’s conversation in Aforeday, and, where Telegram still allows it, deletes the bot’s approval messages from the last 48 hours. Everything else already in the chat stays in Telegram until you delete it.

Exporting your data

In Settings › Privacy & data › Export my data, after a recent sign-in. You get a single JSON file containing your profile, your settings, your sign-in methods, your devices, your subscription, your consent history, your events, your cards, your feedback, your assistant conversations, your briefings, your drafts, your meeting reminders, the actions the agent proposed or took for you with your decisions and their outcomes, a record of each agent run, your sign-in history, and the accounts you connected with the permissions each granted. It also contains, where they apply:

  • the trips Aforeday detected, and the flights you marked as not yours;
  • your Outlook Calendar events, among your events;
  • the facts Aforeday keeps about your Outlook mail conversations, except the messages’ technical identifiers and the links to open them in Outlook;
  • your GitHub notifications;
  • your Linear issues;
  • your Jira issues;
  • your Asana tasks;
  • your Notion tasks, among your events, and the Notion databases you shared;
  • your HubSpot deals, with their amounts, and contacts;
  • your Salesforce opportunities, with their amounts, and contacts;
  • the facts Aforeday keeps about your Slack direct messages, and the promises it found;
  • your linked chats (which chat, the name Telegram showed, the detail setting and when it was linked) and which conversations happened in a chat.

What it leaves out is credentials: no token, and nothing that stands in for one, is ever included (section 11).

Deleting your account

In Settings › Privacy & data › Delete account, after a recent sign-in, by typing DELETE. If you signed in with Apple, the app asks you to confirm with Sign in with Apple once more, so that we can tell Apple to withdraw Aforeday’s access to your Apple ID: the code Apple gives us is exchanged and revoked at once and never stored. Deletion then revokes every session immediately, cancels anything the agent was waiting to do, disconnects every connected account as described above, and permanently deletes your profile, sign-in links, devices and push tokens, calendar events and reminders, everything Aforeday kept from your connected accounts, cards, feedback, briefings, meeting reminders, assistant conversations, drafts, the agent’s records and settings, your trips, and your chat links and chat conversations — then checks that those tables hold nothing of yours. Your phone erases its local copy once the server confirms.

What survives, and why: your consent history and audit records, and the records of the deletion job and of any export you requested — all of them under random identifiers, with nothing left in them that links them to you (see section 9); Apple’s transaction identifiers in our App Store notification records; and anything in a backup, for about two weeks. Messages already in Telegram stay there; deleting your account does not delete them. If you signed in with Google, we hold no Google token to withdraw; you can remove Aforeday from your Google Account, under its list of third-party connections. Deleting your Aforeday account does not cancel your App Store subscription — do that in your iPhone’s Settings, under your name › Subscriptions.

11. Security

Traffic between the app and our servers uses TLS. Our secrets are held in Google Secret Manager; no sign-in token is ever included in an export. On your phone, your sign-in tokens are held in the iOS Keychain, available only after you unlock the device and never synced to iCloud. The access tokens of the accounts you connect exist only on our servers, encrypted with a key held in Secret Manager; they are never sent to your phone or included in an export. Messages that connected services send to our servers to say something changed are checked against that service’s signature or a secret we set before anything is done with them. Our logs pass through a filter that removes addresses, tokens, keys and draft text.

We do not read your calendar, your conversations, what Aforeday reads from your connected accounts, or the data Aforeday derives from them. The exceptions are narrow: investigating a problem you report to us, investigating a security incident, or where the law requires it — and in those cases we work from identifiers and counts wherever that is enough. No part of the app or our API lets anyone see another person’s data, and there is no administrative interface that shows your content.

12. Your rights

You have the right to access your data (Article 15 GDPR), to correct it (16), to have it erased (17), to restrict its processing (18), to receive it in a portable form (20), and to object to processing based on our legitimate interests (21). The export, disconnect and deletion tools in the app cover access, portability and erasure directly; for anything else, or if the export does not include what you need, write to contact@aforeday.com with “Privacy request” in the subject. We answer within one month. If you are not an Aforeday user but appear in an Aforeday user’s data — for example as someone who emailed them — you can write to the same address.

You can also complain to a supervisory authority. Ours is the Berliner Beauftragte für Datenschutz und Informationsfreiheit, Alt-Moabit 59-61, 10555 Berlin.

13. Children

Aforeday is made for working adults and is not directed at children. You must be at least 18 to use it, and we do not knowingly process the data of anyone younger.
