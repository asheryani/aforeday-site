Privacy policy

For the Aforeday iPhone app and this website. Last updated 12 November 2026, version 3.2. It replaces version 3.1 of 3 November 2026. It describes the app as it works on that date, including features that are switched off.

The short version

  • You sign in with Apple or Google. Google is asked only for openid, email, and profile. Identity tokens are checked and then discarded.
  • Calendar and reminders are read on the iPhone through Apple EventKit. Notes, links, attendee email addresses, and coordinates are not uploaded.
  • Location is When In Use only. Coordinates never leave the iPhone. Our servers receive a leave-by time, not a position.
  • Spoken replies send the reply text to Amazon Polly in Frankfurt (eu-central-1). We do not keep that audio. Talk mode is switched off.
  • Text AI is Amazon Nova 2 Lite, model amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0, invoked as the EU inference profile eu.amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0, from eu-central-1.
  • Drafts are prepared on the phone and are not sent as mail. Gmail, Slack, and the other connectors are switched off.
  • Aforeday Pro Monthly (de.neurotechai.purser.pro.monthly) and Pro Annual (de.neurotechai.purser.pro.annual) are billed by Apple. There is no free trial.
  • This website sets no cookies, runs no analytics, and loads nothing from anyone else. The app’s own counts and crash notes are described in section 2.

2. What Aforeday processes

Your account

You sign in with Sign in with Apple or with Google. The phone sends the identity token, a nonce, an optional display name, and a device record to our servers. Google’s authorization-code exchange happens on the phone. The API receives only Google’s identity token. Google is asked only for openid, email, and profile, which do not include Gmail or Google Calendar.

Apple and Google identity tokens are verified and then discarded. They are not stored on the account, the sign-in link, or the session. What we keep from a successful sign-in is the provider (apple or google), the provider’s subject identifier, the email address when the provider returns one, when that link was last used, your email address, and your display name. Apple’s display name is the name the Apple sheet returns. Google’s is the name sent with the request, or the name in the token.

A replay key is kept in Redis: the SHA-256 hash of the raw identity token, the value 1, for the token’s remaining lifetime, and never longer than one hour or shorter than one second. The token text is not stored. If Redis is unavailable, that replay check fails open.

A session has two tokens. The access token is a signed JWT. Its default lifetime is 900 seconds, and the configuration ceiling is 3600 seconds. Its claims are your user id, the device id, the session id, and a family id. The refresh token is 32 random bytes. Only its SHA-256 hash is stored. Its default lifetime is 30 days, and the configuration ceiling is 90 days. When it is rotated, the previous row is kept so reuse can be detected. No job deletes expired or rotated session rows. They are removed when the account is deleted. Sign-in does not store an Apple or Google refresh token. At account deletion only, the app requests a separate Sign in with Apple authorization code, which is exchanged for a refresh token and used to revoke Apple’s grant. That refresh token is not written to the session table.

The device record written at sign-in is the platform (ios), the generic model name the phone reports (such as “iPhone”, not a hardware identifier), the iOS version, the app version, and when the device was last seen. A later update can add the push token, the Live Activity push-to-start token, whether notifications are allowed, whether Live Activities are enabled, the push environment, and a refreshed iOS or app version. The device row has a locale field. Sign-in does not set it.

On the iPhone, the access token and the refresh token are one Keychain item, under the service de.neurotechai.purser.auth and the account name session-tokens. The item holds the access token, the refresh token, when the access token expires, your user id, and the device id. It is available after the first unlock following a restart, and only on this iPhone. It is not synced to iCloud Keychain, and it is not restored onto another device. A flag that you need to sign in again is stored in the app’s settings and is not a token.

Spoken replies, and Talk mode

With Talk mode off, holding to speak is transcribed on the iPhone. The phone uses an on-device speech model when it has one for the language. Otherwise it uses Apple’s speech recognizer with on-device recognition required. If the phone cannot do that language on the device, capture does not start, and the audio is not sent to Apple. The transcript text can be sent to us as your message. The recording is not.

Spoken replies are on. The reply text is sent to Amazon Polly in Frankfurt (eu-central-1) and returned as an audio stream. We do not keep that audio. It is not written to our storage, and the permission used for Polly does not include storing audio. If Frankfurt does not produce audio within about 1.5 seconds, or the audio is empty or fails, the iPhone reads the reply with its own voice. Talk mode is switched off, so the live voice session is not active. That session would have used Amazon Nova 2 Sonic, model amazon.nova-2-sonic-v1:0, in Stockholm (eu-north-1). It is deployed and not running. Amazon Transcribe, which is only a fallback while Talk mode is on, is not used.

Calendar and reminders

With your permission, the app reads the calendars and reminder lists you choose through Apple EventKit, on the iPhone, and uploads a minimized record. You can change that choice in the app or in the iPhone’s Settings. Deselecting a calendar, or revoking access, clears the on-device cache and sends identifiers only, so the server can retract those events.

For a calendar event, the upload can include a stable id, the occurrence time of a repeating event, the store’s own identifier, the calendar’s external identifier, the title, attendee display names, start, end, whether it is all day, the time zone, a timestamp, a fixed sensitivity of “medium”, a confidence of 1, and a deletion status. The summary, the source link, and the raw payload reference are empty on this path. Notes, links, attendee email addresses, the organizer’s address, and coordinates are read from EventKit and are not uploaded. The location string, and the yes/no facts about who organized the event and whether the invitation is still open, stay on the iPhone and are not part of the uploaded record.

For a reminder, the upload is the title, the due date, the due date’s time zone, and an empty participant list. Incomplete reminders are included. A completed reminder is included only when it was completed after the import cutoff. The list’s title, colour, and account name stay in the on-device calendar selection.

Your location

Location is used for place-based reminders and for when to leave. It is When In Use only. The app never asks for Always. If a profile has granted Always, the app treats it as When In Use. There is no background location mode. Updates are significant-change monitoring only, and only while a located event is inside a three-hour departure window. Accuracy is requested at 100 metres. One temporary full-accuracy request can run, under the purpose “Imminent departure”, when a departure is imminent and you granted approximate location.

Coordinates never leave the iPhone. The last fix is one position on the phone, replaced rather than added to a history, excluded from backup, and discarded after 24 hours. A fix older than 15 minutes is not used as a starting point. Home and work addresses are kept on the phone and are not part of the upload. Directions, geocoding, and weather run on the iPhone, through Apple’s MapKit, WeatherKit, and geocoder. A place name for a reminder is geocoded on the iPhone.

What we receive is not a coordinate. Turning the setting on or off sends a consent record: the type of consent, the version location-1, whether it was granted, and the surface. A leave-by row can also be uploaded with the other device events: the title is “Leave by” or “Leave by” plus a weather class (clear, rain, heavy rain, snow, or severe), the start is the leave-by time, the end is the meeting’s start, and the meeting’s id is attached. It has no address and no coordinate. The weather class is defined so it cannot name a place. Which origin was used (your position, the previous event, or a saved address) stays on the phone. No route accepts a latitude or longitude.

Consent records are an append-only ledger: the type, the version, whether it was granted, when, the surface, and an optional detail. Nothing deletes them on a schedule. They stay for the life of the account. When you delete the account, the user id on each row is replaced with a random identifier that is stored nowhere else, and the row is kept with no further expiry. The same replacement is applied to the audit records of an entitlement, the account, a privacy action, an agent action, a draft, an agent run, and a channel link. The record of the deletion job itself is kept under a different random identifier.

Focus, Live Activities, and what is switched off

A focus session is created on the iPhone: a local record titled “Focus”, the reason “You chose to focus.”, the start and end, a snapshot in the app group (or in Application Support if that container is missing), and a local notification for the end. The activity is started on the phone. The “session started” count stays on the phone. It is not in the set of counts posted to our servers.

Live Activities are on. The phone registers a push-to-start token and a token for each activity. We store those tokens. Our servers can start a meeting or a travel activity in the discreet style, with the meeting title, the reason “Starting soon” or “Time to leave soon”, the start and end, a priority band, and the actions complete and snooze. Focus activities are started on the phone, not by that push.

Evening close-out is switched off and does not run. Share capture is switched off: the share extension does not call our servers, and the app reads a local inbox only while a switch that defaults to off is on. Promise catcher, cost of yes, and worth it are also switched off.

Counts and crash notes

The app does not include a third-party analytics, crash, or advertising SDK. A closed set of product counts is posted to our servers and stored as daily totals with no user id. A crash note can be posted and is written as one log line, not a database row. It can include the kind of failure, the exception name, the reason, stack symbols, and the build. That upload is on by default and can be turned off under Settings › Diagnostics. Apple’s MetricKit payloads stay on the device.

Your subscription

Aforeday Pro is bought through Apple’s App Store. There is no free trial. The products the phone offers are Pro Monthly, de.neurotechai.purser.pro.monthly, and Pro Annual, de.neurotechai.purser.pro.annual. A further product id exists in the server configuration and is not shown: the phone defaults that product to hidden. Apple processes the payment. We verify the purchase with Apple’s App Store Server API. The signed transaction is checked and is not stored.

For your subscription we keep one row: the tier, the product id, Apple’s original transaction id, the status (none, active, grace, billing retry, expired, or revoked), when it expires, when a grace period expires, whether it renews, whether the environment is sandbox or production, the time of the last notification (used only to order events), when we last verified it, and when the row was created and updated. There is no price, currency, receipt, or Apple account token. Deleting the account deletes this row.

Each App Store notification is stored as a summary with no user id and no payment content: Apple’s notification id, the type, the subtype, the original transaction id, the environment, Apple’s signed date, the outcome, and when we processed it. Those rows are kept after the account is deleted, because they are not tied to a user.

3. Drafts and approval

The draft editor does not send mail. It tries the Gmail app, then a mailto: link, and if neither opens it copies the text. An event action can also hand a mailto: or sms: link to the system. The phone does not read a mailbox. There is no in-app mail or message composer.

The screen “What Aforeday may do on its own” has two choices, “Ask me first” and “Act and tell me”. Choosing “Act and tell me” asks for Face ID or the passcode. That choice is stored with your preferences. A server path for sending an email reply or a Slack reply is switched on at the agent, and it has nothing to send with: the Gmail and Slack connectors are switched off, and Slack sending defaults off. In this launch, that path does not deliver mail or Slack messages.

4. AI and spoken replies

Text is handled by Amazon Bedrock in the task’s region, eu-central-1 (Frankfurt). The model id is amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0. The call uses the EU inference profile eu.amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0. Permission to invoke the model is limited to that profile and to the foundation model when the profile matches. A request is rejected if it does not match the task’s fixed shape. Before the call, labelled secrets, credential-like strings, one-time codes, and card- or IBAN-shaped numbers are replaced. The prompt and the model’s raw completion are not written to the database and are not logged on the hosted service.

What can be included, depending on the task: one message’s text (up to 8,000 characters) with its author role, counterparty, and time; up to 12 message texts for a thread summary; a meeting title, time, up to 30 attendee names, a location string of up to 200 characters, up to 10 earlier events, and open commitment texts; up to six short sections of a daily brief; your assistant message (up to 2,000 characters), up to 40 earlier turns, and up to 24 titles from your day; a draft’s title, reason, participant addresses, and commitment texts, without a mail body; a calendar reply’s title, times, names, your reason, an optional note of up to 300 characters, and up to three proposed windows; or a card’s title and reason and, only when a thread was read, up to 15 message texts. Research briefs, which would include page text, are switched off.

Each call stores a technical row: the task name, the prompt and model versions, token counts, cost, latency, and whether it succeeded. The row has no prompt text and no user id. A comment on that table mentions 13 months. No job enforces that period, so this policy does not promise it. The rows are not deleted when an account is deleted. Results you can see, such as a brief, a draft, or an assistant message, are stored in their own tables, which section 9 times separately.

You can turn this off under Settings › Privacy › Privacy & data › AI data controls, with the switch “AI enrichment”. Off, Aforeday uses only its fixed rules and no content is sent to the model. While it is on, three further switches can leave out email content, calendar content, or assistant conversations. A separate switch, “Turn off Aforeday's agent”, is the agent’s own opt-in and is not this control.

Spoken replies, described in section 2, use Amazon Polly in eu-central-1. We do not keep the audio.

5. What is not active

Signing in with Google does not connect Gmail or Google Calendar. Calendar and reminders come from the iPhone through EventKit.

These connectors are switched off and receive nothing in this launch: Gmail (including message bodies), Google Calendar, Outlook calendar, Outlook mail, Slack, Notion, GitHub, Linear, Jira, Asana, HubSpot, Salesforce, Telegram, and WhatsApp. Also switched off: web research, check-ins, booking, and flight-status lookup. The related polls are created and disabled. Talk mode, evening close-out, Share capture, promise catcher, cost of yes, and worth it are switched off.

Trip detection is on. It is a fixed check of calendar events already stored, not a call to a flight-status service. The phone has a “Detected trips” screen. Flight alerts are on unless you turn them off. Turning them off deletes your trips on the next sweep. An external flight-status provider is not used.

6. Why, and on what legal basis

  • To provide the app — the processing in sections 2 to 4. Legal basis: performance of our contract with you, Article 6(1)(b) GDPR.
  • Other people in your data — names of people on the calendars and reminders you allow, and names in drafts you ask for. We process them only so Aforeday can show your day and prepare a draft for you. Legal basis: the legitimate interest, yours and ours, in providing the service you asked for, Article 6(1)(f) GDPR.
  • To keep the service secure and working — logs, abuse prevention, and request limits. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in a secure, functioning service, Article 6(1)(f) GDPR.
  • To prove we honoured your choices — records of consent and of deletion, where we keep them. Legal basis: our legal obligation to demonstrate compliance, Article 6(1)(c) with Article 5(2) GDPR.
  • To sell and service subscriptions — the subscription records Apple’s notifications allow us to keep. Legal basis: performance of our contract, and our legal obligations in accounting.

We do not use your data for advertising, we do not sell it, and we do not make automated decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects. Suggestions and drafts can be wrong. They do not decide anything for you.

7. Who else sees it

  • Amazon Web Services, in eu-central-1 unless a row in section 8 says otherwise. The application runs on ECS Fargate, behind a load balancer. The database is RDS for PostgreSQL, named aforeday. The cache is ElastiCache Redis. Secrets Manager holds operational secrets (signing keys, the database password, the Redis credential, the token-encryption key, the internal tick secret, the push key, Apple root certificates, and the Sign in with Apple key). Logs go to CloudWatch. EventBridge calls internal jobs. The network is a VPC with private subnets and a NAT. Text AI is Amazon Bedrock Nova 2 Lite, as section 4 describes. Spoken replies use Amazon Polly in eu-central-1. There is no production object-storage bucket: the only S3 bucket in the configuration is for a spike profile, and that profile is not created in production.
  • Apple — Sign in with Apple (the phone shows Apple’s sheet; we fetch Apple’s signing keys, and on deletion we can call Apple to revoke the grant), push for alerts and Live Activities, and the App Store Server API to verify a purchase. EventKit, MapKit, WeatherKit, Core Location, and geocoding run on the iPhone inside Apple’s frameworks. Coordinates are not sent to us. MetricKit diagnostics stay on the device. Choosing “Act and tell me” asks the phone for Face ID or the passcode.
  • Google, for sign-in only — the phone completes Google’s sign-in, and we verify the identity token against Google’s published certificates. The permissions are only those in section 2. Gmail and Google Calendar are switched off.

No advertising SDK is included. The counts and crash notes in section 2 are first-party. The connectors in section 5 are in the code and switched off, so they receive nothing while they stay off.

8. Where it is processed

The application, database, cache, secrets, logs, Amazon Polly, and the Bedrock call are in Frankfurt, Germany (eu-central-1). Text uses model amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0 through the EU inference profile eu.amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0. A request sent from Frankfurt under that profile may be served in any of these six regions: Frankfurt (eu-central-1), Stockholm (eu-north-1), Milan (eu-south-1), Spain (eu-south-2), Ireland (eu-west-1), and Paris (eu-west-3). London is not one of them. The global profile that includes London is not used. Nova 2 Sonic (amazon.nova-2-sonic-v1:0) is in Stockholm and is switched off with Talk mode. Amazon Transcribe in eu-central-1 is that mode’s fallback and is not used while Talk mode is off.

Apple’s sign-in, push, and App Store services, and Google’s sign-in, are operated by those companies under their own terms and may involve processing outside the EU. Where that happens, the transfer takes place on the transfer mechanisms in that provider’s terms, such as the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses.

10. Exporting and deleting

Export and deletion are on one screen, titled “Privacy & data”, opened from Settings › Privacy. “Export my data” asks our servers for an export, waits for the job, and saves aforeday-export-<day>.json on the iPhone. The download link lasts 900 seconds. “Delete account” asks you to type DELETE. The button “Delete everything” stays disabled until the field is exactly DELETE. “Keep my account” cancels. Export and deletion both require a recent sign-in, within 900 seconds.

The export is one JSON file, held for the download link and not as a database table. It includes your profile, preferences, consent records, events, recommendations, feedback, commitments, meeting briefs, daily briefings, drafts, sign-in methods, devices, sessions, and the subscription row when you have one. It also includes assistant conversations, session timers, agent actions, and agent runs. Push tokens, account tokens, and the refresh-token hash are left out. Sections for connected accounts, email threads, Slack, GitHub, trackers, Notion, chat links, research briefs, and CRM deals and contacts are part of the same file. Those connectors are switched off, so those sections are empty in this launch. Trip rows are included without the raw provider payload.

Deleting the account erases, for that user, push records, Live Activity tokens, devices, events, session timers, connector state (email, Slack, Notion, GitHub, trackers, CRM), trips, research briefs, agent actions and runs, recommendations, feedback, commitments, briefs, assistant conversations and their messages, drafts, integration accounts and tokens, idempotency records, sync markers, preferences, channel links, the subscription row, sign-in links, and then the user. Sessions are revoked and removed with the user. Queued push jobs for that user are removed. If you signed in with Apple, deletion can also revoke Apple’s grant, using an authorization code that is not written to the session table.

What remains is described in section 9: consent and audit records, and the deletion job, each under a random identifier; App Store notification summaries, technical AI call records, and product counts, which have no user id. Deleting the Aforeday account does not cancel the App Store subscription. Cancel that in the iPhone’s Settings, under your name, then Subscriptions. There is no screen titled “Security”. The neighbouring screen is “Data & permissions”, which shows the status of calendar, reminders, notifications, and location. The API can list sessions. No iPhone screen was found that lists or revokes other sessions.

11. Security

Traffic between the app and our servers uses TLS. Operational secrets are in AWS Secrets Manager, as section 7 lists. Choosing “Act and tell me” asks the iPhone for Face ID or the passcode. There is no iPhone screen that shows another person’s content, and no screen was found that lists other sessions.

12. Your rights

You have the right to access your data (Article 15 GDPR), to correct it (16), to have it erased (17), to restrict its processing (18), to receive it in a portable form (20), and to object to processing based on our legitimate interests (21). Export and deletion in the app are how you use access, portability, and erasure directly. For anything else, write to contact@aforeday.com with “Privacy request” in the subject. We answer within one month.

You can also complain to a supervisory authority. Ours is the Berliner Beauftragte für Datenschutz und Informationsfreiheit, Alt-Moabit 59-61, 10555 Berlin, Germany.

13. Children

Aforeday is not directed at children. You must be at least 18 to use it, and we do not knowingly process the data of anyone younger.

14. This website

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics, embeds no third-party fonts or scripts, and stores nothing about you. Fonts are the Apple system font or Inter files served from this site. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, which receives your IP address and the request in its logs in order to deliver the pages, under GitHub’s privacy statement.

15. Changes

If Aforeday starts collecting something new, or uses what it has for a new purpose, we update this policy first and change the date at the top. Where the app shows a notice of a new version, it records that you have seen it.
