Privacy policy

For the Purser AI iPhone app and this website. Effective 17 September 2026, version 1.0. It describes the app as it actually works today, not as we intend it to work.

The short version

  • Purser reads what you connect: your iPhone’s calendar and reminders, and optionally Gmail, Google Calendar and Slack.
  • Of your mail, only the messages you sent are read as text, to find promises in them. The message is never stored — the promise, the sentence it came from, the person and the due date are.
  • For other mail, only who wrote to whom and when. Never subjects, never the text.
  • AI runs on Google Cloud in the EU. Nothing is used to train models. You can switch AI off.
  • Purser never sends anything from your accounts. Drafts open in your mail app for you to send.
  • No advertising, no tracking, no data brokers, no third-party analytics.
  • You can export your data or delete your account from inside the app.

2. What Purser processes

Your account

You sign in with Apple or with Google. We store the email address the provider gives us (for Apple this may be a private relay address), your name if it is provided, the provider’s account identifier, and a record of each device that signs in: model, iOS version, app version and when it was last seen. Sign-in sessions are stored as a random token, kept only as a hash, valid 30 days and replaced each time it is used.

Your iPhone’s calendars and reminders

With your permission, the app reads the calendars and reminder lists you include — on first run, all visible ones — and uploads, for each event, its title, start and end, time zone, all-day flag and the display names of its attendees, and for each reminder its title and due date. Events from 90 days back to 60 days ahead are included, along with reminders that are open or were completed in the last 7 days.

Notes, event URLs and locations are not uploaded. Organiser and attendee email addresses from your iPhone’s calendar are reduced on the device to three yes/no facts: whether you organised the meeting, whether the organiser is outside your organisation, and whether your invitation is still unanswered. Short phrases derived from those facts, such as “booked by someone outside your organisation”, are uploaded as part of the cards Purser shows you.

Addresses you enter for travel planning, and the locations of your events, stay on your iPhone. They are sent from your phone to Apple Maps and Apple Weather to work out when to leave and whether weather will delay you; only the resulting “leave by” time and the meeting it belongs to reach us. The app never asks for your device location, contacts, photos, camera or health data. Voice input is transcribed on your device; the audio is never uploaded, and the text is sent only if you send it.

What you create in Purser

Commitments you confirm or that are extracted from your messages; the cards Purser shows you and what you do with them; your conversations with the assistant, including the replies and the items they cite; drafts you ask for, including any edits you make; meeting briefs and daily briefings; and your settings.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, we store your device’s push tokens. Notifications created on your device follow the preview mode you choose there — full details, a discreet summary, or only that Purser has something for you. Purser also starts a Lock Screen activity before a meeting; that message is composed on our servers and currently always uses discreet wording (“Upcoming meeting”), because the app does not send your preview choice to us. We keep a record of each push we send — its type, status and time, never its text — and, for the meeting reminders, the meeting’s title and time.

Purchases

If you subscribe, Apple handles the payment; we never see your card or billing address. We store the signed receipt Apple gives us, your subscription’s status, product, expiry and Apple’s transaction identifier.

Crash reports and logs

When the app crashes, it writes a note — the kind of crash, the exception or signal, a reason and up to 64 lines of the program’s internal call stack — and uploads it automatically the next time you open the app. A failed dictation also produces a note, which can include the name of the audio device in use, such as a named pair of headphones. These notes go into our server logs, not our database. Our logs otherwise hold internal identifiers, counts, error descriptions and, for requests we throttle, IP addresses. Purser’s code does not write the content of your mail or calendar to its logs.

3. Your Google data, permission by permission

Connecting a Google account is optional; Purser works with your iPhone’s own calendar and reminders without it. When you connect, the app first shows you every permission with the explanation below, then opens Google’s consent page, where you can decline. Signing in with Google, rather than connecting an account, requests only openid, email and profile. You can disconnect at any time in Settings › Integrations › Connected accounts.

openid, email, profile — which account this is

We read Google’s account identifier, your email address and your name. For a connected account we store the identifier and the email address, which is what Settings shows you. For Sign in with Google we also store your name.

calendar.readonly — your meetings, read only

We read the list of your calendars and store their names; your primary calendar is selected unless you choose others. For each calendar you select we read events from 30 days back to 60 days ahead, and store per event: the title, the first 500 characters of the description, start and end, time zone, all-day flag, the organiser’s and attendees’ email addresses, which calendar it belongs to, and the link to it in Google Calendar. Locations, conference details, attachments and attendee names are dropped. Purser holds no write permission: it cannot create, change or delete an event. The only thing it writes to Google Calendar is its own notification channel, so Google can tell it when your calendar changes.

gmail.metadata — who is waiting on whom

We read your mailbox address, each message’s labels, and only these headers: From, To, Cc, Date, Message-ID, the threading headers, and the mailing-list and automatic-reply headers. This permission cannot return the content of a message, and Purser never searches your mailbox. It covers your most recent Inbox and Sent messages and, after that, new messages as they arrive.

What we store per conversation: the Gmail thread identifier, who wrote last and when, the other participants’ email addresses, whether you were a direct recipient, whether you had replied before, how many messages there are, and whether it looks like a newsletter — plus the follow-up cards built from it (“Reply to …”, “Waiting on …”) with a link to the thread. Subjects, snippets and message text are never read or stored.

gmail.readonly — the promises in mail you sent

This is the permission that lets Purser find the commitments in your own messages. It is used by exactly one part of our code, for exactly one purpose, and a test in our build fails if that ever stops being true.

  • Which messages. Only messages labelled Sent and sent from the connected address, newest first, at most 25 per synchronisation.
  • What is read. The text of one part of the message, limited to the first 8,000 characters after lines that quote earlier messages are removed. Quoting inside HTML formatting is not always recognised, so an HTML-only message may be used as it is.
  • What is never done with it. Purser does not use this permission to read the text of mail you received, attachments or drafts. It does not touch Drive, Contacts or any other Google service, and it holds no permission to send, delete, label or change anything.
  • What happens to the text. It exists in memory for one analysis and is never written to our database or our logs. If you have switched AI processing off, or opted email out of it, the text is discarded without being analysed at all.
  • What is stored. For each promise found: a short description of it, the exact sentence it came from, who it was to, the due date, how confident the model was, whether it is still open, a link to the message, and the reminder card that quotes the sentence. If you mark a sentence “not a promise”, we keep that sentence so it is not suggested again.
  • One thing to know. When the model is highly confident a sentence is a promise, it is added to your open commitments without asking you first. Everything less confident waits for you to confirm it.

4. Slack

If you connect Slack, Purser reads your direct-message conversations — up to 200 of them, the 15 most recent messages in each — and the display names of the people in them. It stores, per conversation, the channel identifier, who spoke last and when, the other person’s Slack identifier and display name, and whether you had replied before. Message text is not stored. Your own direct messages from the last 7 days (up to 25 per synchronisation, up to 8,000 characters) are analysed for promises in the same way as your sent mail. Purser requests the ability to search your messages but does not use it; we are removing that permission.

5. AI processing

Purser uses Google’s Gemini 2.5 Flash model through Google Cloud Vertex AI, in Google’s europe-west1 region (Belgium). It is used for six things: finding promises in messages you sent, writing meeting briefs and daily briefings, answering you in the assistant, and writing reply drafts and meeting decline or reschedule notes you ask for.

What is sent is limited to what the feature needs: one message; or a meeting with up to ten earlier meetings involving the same people, related mail counterparts and open promises; or short titles of your open items and upcoming events. Never your whole mailbox or calendar. Before anything is sent, common signature endings and tracking links are removed, the length is capped, and pattern filters replace common secret formats — labelled passwords and tokens, API keys, one-time codes, card- and IBAN-shaped numbers — with placeholders. Requests that exceed the limits are refused rather than trimmed.

Prompts and responses are not written to our logs. We keep a technical record of each AI call — the task, the model and prompt versions, token counts, cost, how long it took and whether it succeeded — with no content and no link to you.

Under our agreement with Google, this data is processed by Google as our service provider and is not used to train Google’s models. We do not use it to train any model of our own, and no part of our code trains or fine-tunes a model.

You can switch AI off entirely in Settings › Privacy › Privacy & data, or switch off individual sources: email content, calendar content, or assistant conversations. With AI off, Purser falls back to its fixed rules, which is how it decides what needs your attention in any case. Note that switching AI off does not stop the mail and Slack synchronisation itself: the text of your sent messages is still fetched, and then discarded without being analysed.

6. Why, and on what legal basis

  • To provide the app — everything in sections 2 to 5. Legal basis: performance of our contract with you, Article 6(1)(b) GDPR.
  • To connect an account you choose — reading Gmail, Google Calendar or Slack. Legal basis: performance of our contract, on the permissions you grant at the provider and can withdraw there or in the app.
  • To keep the service secure and working — logs, crash reports, request throttling, abuse prevention. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in a secure, functioning service, Article 6(1)(f) GDPR.
  • To prove we honoured your choices — the consent and audit records described in section 9. Legal basis: our legal obligation to demonstrate compliance, Article 6(1)(c) with Article 5(2) GDPR.
  • To sell and service subscriptions — the receipt and subscription records. Legal basis: performance of our contract.

We do not use your data for advertising or profiling, we do not sell it, and we do not make automated decisions about you with legal or similarly significant effects.

7. Who else sees it

  • Google Cloud — as our processor, for hosting (Cloud Run), our database (Cloud SQL), short-lived queues and caches (Memorystore), AI processing (Vertex AI), secret storage, scheduling and logs.
  • Google — as the source you authorise, for Gmail and Calendar, and as a sign-in provider.
  • Slack — as the source you authorise, if you connect it.
  • Apple — for Sign in with Apple, push notifications, and the App Store when you subscribe. From your phone only: Apple Maps and Apple Weather for travel planning, and on-device speech recognition.
  • Where you send it yourself — when you open a draft, it goes to your mail app with its recipients, subject and body, and you send it.

There is no advertising network, no analytics provider, no crash-reporting service and no data broker in Purser. The app contains no third-party code at all.

8. Where it is processed

The app’s servers, database, caches and AI processing run in Google Cloud’s europe-west1 region in Belgium. Where a transfer outside the EU occurs, it takes place on the transfer mechanisms in our providers’ terms, such as the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses.

10. Disconnecting, exporting and deleting

Disconnecting an account

In Settings › Integrations › Connected accounts. Disconnecting asks the provider to revoke our access, destroys the stored tokens whether or not the provider confirms, records the withdrawal of each permission, deletes that account’s Gmail conversation summaries and its commitments, and hides its calendar events and follow-up cards at once. Hidden events are erased by the 90-day job; hidden cards when you delete your account. A record that the account was connected — its address and your calendar names — is kept until you delete your account. You can also revoke Purser at myaccount.google.com/permissions; syncing then stops, and nothing is deleted automatically.

Exporting your data

In Settings › Privacy › Privacy & data › Export my data, after a recent sign-in. You get a single JSON file containing your profile, your settings, your connected accounts, your consent history, your events, your cards, your feedback, your commitments, your briefings and your drafts. It does not yet include your assistant conversations, your mail and Slack conversation summaries, or the quoted sentences behind your commitments; if you want those, ask us and we will provide them.

Deleting your account

In Settings › Privacy › Privacy & data › Delete account, after a recent sign-in, by typing DELETE. This revokes every session immediately, disconnects every connected account, and permanently deletes your profile, sign-in links, devices and push tokens, connected accounts and tokens, calendar events, mail summaries, cards, feedback, commitments, briefings, assistant conversations, drafts and settings — then checks that those tables hold nothing of yours. Your phone erases its local copy once the server confirms.

What survives, and why: your consent history and audit records, under a random identifier (see section 9); the records of the deletion job and of any export you requested, which keep your former internal account identifier; Apple’s transaction identifiers in our App Store notification records; the meeting reminder records described in section 9, which we are changing; your Slack conversation summaries, which age out within 72 hours; and anything in a backup, for about eight days. Deleting your Purser account does not cancel an App Store subscription — do that in your iPhone’s Settings — and does not withdraw your Sign in with Apple grant, which you can remove under Settings › your name › Sign in with Apple.

11. Security

Traffic between the app and our servers uses TLS. Google and Slack tokens are stored encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a per-record key, itself encrypted by a key held in Google Secret Manager; no token is ever returned to the app or included in an export. On your phone, your sign-in tokens are held in the iOS Keychain, available only after you unlock the device and never synced to iCloud. Our logs pass through a filter that removes addresses, tokens, keys and draft text.

We do not read your mail, your calendar, or the data Purser derives from them. The exceptions are narrow: investigating a problem you report to us, investigating a security incident, or where the law requires it — and in those cases we work from identifiers and counts wherever that is enough. No part of the app or our API lets anyone see another person’s data, and there is no administrative interface that shows your content.

Purser’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Specifically: we use Google user data only to provide and improve the user-facing features described in this policy; we do not transfer it except as needed to provide those features, for security purposes, or to comply with applicable law; we do not allow humans to read it except in the narrow cases in section 11; and we never use or transfer it for advertising, credit decisions, or to train generalised AI models.

The export and deletion tools in the app cover access, portability and erasure directly; for anything else, or if the export does not include what you need, write to hello@neurotech-ai.de with “Privacy request” in the subject. We answer within one month.

You can also complain to a supervisory authority. Ours is the Berliner Beauftragte für Datenschutz und Informationsfreiheit, Alt-Moabit 59-61, 10555 Berlin.
